THE SHAPES WE FIND IN THE DARK

Constellations

Patterns above the Known World, some familiar, some forgotten, and some that should not be there.

Reading the Sky

Constellations are recorded as observed star patterns, cultural interpretations, and disputed arrangements. Their names and meanings may vary between civilizations.

NOCTURNE maintains separate records for established astronomical constellations and fictional patterns associated with the Veil.

PATTERN / C-001 / VERIFIED WITHIN THE SETTING

The Waking Stag

Appearance: A branching arrangement of stars resembling the outline of a stag with raised antlers.

Associated region: Northern Known World.

Folklore: Said to appear above forests before the first snowfall.

Observation note: The pattern is consistent across surviving charts, though its oldest name is unknown.

PATTERN / C-002 / DISPUTED

The Drowned Crown

Appearance: Seven faint stars arranged in an incomplete circle.

Associated region: Southern Veil-facing coastlines.

Folklore: A crown lost beneath a black sea, visible only to those who have forgotten something important.

Observation note: Several charts contain this pattern, but the star positions do not match.

PATTERN / C-003 / UNCONFIRMED

The Hollow Witness

Appearance: A circle of stars surrounding an apparently empty center.

Associated region: Unknown.

Folklore: Believed to represent an observer who watches from beyond the sky.

Observation note: No two researchers agree on the number of stars in the formation.

PATTERN / C-004 / UNRESOLVED

The Last Lantern

Appearance: A single bright point appearing near the edge of a familiar star pattern.

Associated region: Multiple expedition reports.

Folklore: A light that follows travelers who have lost their way.

Observation note: The object has not been identified or consistently located.

Constellation Research

Every pattern requires a chart, date, location, observation conditions, and a clear distinction between measured star positions and cultural interpretation.

The stars do not know the names we give them.
