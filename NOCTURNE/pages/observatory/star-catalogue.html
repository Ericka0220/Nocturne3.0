OBSERVATORY / STAR CATALOGUE / 061

EVERY LIGHT HAS A PLACE. OR SO WE THOUGHT.

Star Catalogue

A growing index of celestial objects, recorded positions, and unidentified lights.

Catalogue Method

Each entry includes a designation, recorded appearance, observed position, date of observation, and confidence level. Objects with insufficient information remain unidentified rather than receiving invented classifications.

STAR / SC-001 / VERIFIED WITHIN THE SETTING

Vesper

Appearance: A bright, pale-gold celestial body.

Recorded region: Western sky.

Associated pattern: The Waking Stag.

Notes: Used as a seasonal reference point in several regional calendars.

STAR / SC-002 / UNCONFIRMED

Morvane

Appearance: A dim red point of light.

Recorded region: Northern sky, though coordinates differ between reports.

Associated pattern: The Drowned Crown.

Notes: Some historical charts include the object, while others omit it entirely.

OBJECT / SC-003 / UNRESOLVED

Object Without a Name

Appearance: A white light that appears to remain stationary while surrounding stars shift.

Recorded region: Unknown.

Associated pattern: None established.

Notes: Only one complete observation survives.

OBJECT / SC-004 / DISPUTED

The Black Star

Appearance: A dark circular region described as an absence of light.

Recorded region: Unknown.

Associated pattern: The Hollow Witness.

Notes: It is unclear whether the account describes a celestial object, an optical effect, or a symbolic illustration.

Unidentified Objects

Unidentified entries are retained in the catalogue until sufficient evidence supports classification. Researchers may add new observations without deleting earlier reports.
