THE LAWS WE HAVE NOT FINISHED LEARNING

Magic Systems

A record of the structures believed to govern magical phenomena.

What Makes a System?

A magical system is a repeatable pattern of interaction between a source of power and an observable effect. Researchers classify systems by how they appear to function, not by assumptions about their origin.

Two events may look identical while operating under entirely different rules.

Elements of a System

Source

Where the effect appears to originate. Possibilities include living beings, natural environments, artifacts, celestial events, or contact with the Veil.

Method

The conditions or process through which an effect is produced. The method may be symbolic, biological, environmental, or unknown.

Cost

The energy, material, time, memory, or other consequence associated with an effect. Some costs are immediate. Others appear later.

Limitations

Conditions that prevent an effect, weaken it, or restrict its range and duration.

Consequences

Unintended outcomes, environmental changes, physical effects, or lingering disturbances.

Current Research Categories

  • Resonant: Effects associated with sound, vibration, or repeated patterns.
  • Vital: Effects connected to living organisms and biological processes.
  • Symbolic: Effects associated with written marks, shapes, and constructed patterns.
  • Environmental: Effects tied to particular locations or natural conditions.
  • Veil-touched: Effects that appear to involve contact with the Veil.
  • Unclassified: Evidence is insufficient to assign a category.

Classification describes what we observe. It does not explain why it happens.
