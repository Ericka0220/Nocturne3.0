UNWRITTEN / LOST GEOGRAPHY / 068

PLACES THAT REFUSE TO BE FOUND

Lost Locations

Settlements, structures, and regions that appear in accounts but cannot be reliably located.

When a Place Disappears

A lost location may have been abandoned, renamed, destroyed, misrepresented, or incorrectly placed on a map. Some fictional accounts suggest that certain locations connected to the Veil may be difficult to reach or observe consistently.

Each location is documented using surviving geographical descriptions, historical references, maps, and witness accounts.

LOCATION / LL-001 / UNCONFIRMED

The Village of Greywake

Description: A small settlement said to stand beside a river that no longer appears on regional maps.

Last known reference: A travel diary dated several generations before the current archive.

Reported features: Stone houses, a bell tower, and a road lined with white trees.

Search history: Two expeditions failed to identify a matching settlement.

LOCATION / LL-002 / DISPUTED

The House at the End of Winter

Description: A solitary house reportedly appearing along different forest roads.

Last known reference: Three unrelated witness accounts.

Reported features: A dark roof, a frozen garden, and a single illuminated window.

Search history: No consistent geographical location established.

LOCATION / LL-003 / UNRESOLVED

The Staircase Below the Sea

Description: A stone staircase reportedly descending beneath the waterline.

Last known reference: An incomplete coastal expedition record.

Reported features: Carved stone steps and a doorway beneath the tide.

Search history: The original coastline has changed, making identification difficult.

LOCATION / LL-004 / LEGENDARY

The City Without a Shadow

Description: A city said to appear beyond a distant mountain range.

Last known reference: Regional folklore and an anonymous map illustration.

Reported features: Pale towers, empty streets, and sunlight without shadows.

Search history: No verified evidence of the city's existence.

Mapping the Missing

Researchers should retain approximate locations, source dates, route descriptions, environmental details, and all failed search attempts. A failed search is still useful geographical information.

If the road ends, record where it ends. Do not draw the road beyond it.
